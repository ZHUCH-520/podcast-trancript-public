What happens next

Send the source.
It handles the rest in six steps.

Every step stays auditable. The original audio and rough transcript remain available, and uncertain names are marked instead of invented.

01 / INPUT

Identify the source

Use an episode link, audio, video, or an existing transcript. Say whether you want English, Chinese, or both.

02 / PRESERVE

Keep the originals

Each episode gets its own folder. Source media and rough transcripts remain available for later checks.

03 / TRANSCRIBE

Turn speech into text

The skill checks your computer and shows the runtime, model, download size, storage location, and network activity before setting anything up.

04 / VERIFY

Check names, split safely

Guest names and terminology are checked against the episode page. Long transcripts are split losslessly with the bundled script.

05 / EDIT

Clean up each chunk

Punctuation, speaker labels, and obvious ASR errors are fixed without cutting the substance.

06 / EXPORT

Merge, check, export

The beginning, ending, gaps, duplicates, and formatting are checked before creating `transcript.md` and `transcript.html`.

What it protects

Readable, without sanding off
the speaker's voice.

Spoken language can be tidied up. The point and the tone stay. Anything uncertain goes into Unresolved Items for you to review.

01 · COMPLETE

Nothing important goes missing

Claims, examples, and caveats stay in. A summary may help you enter the piece, but it never replaces the full transcript.

02 · GROUNDED

Names aren't guessed

Names are checked when a reliable source exists. Otherwise the transcript uses a neutral label for your review.

03 · HUMAN

The voice still sounds familiar

Useful pauses, humor, and speaking habits remain. Only obvious recognition noise is removed.

04 · PORTABLE

The files are ready to use

You get `transcript.md` for editing and `transcript.html` for reading or sharing, plus Unresolved Items only when something needs checking.

Your first audio or video

See the setup plan.
Then choose what to download.

The skill checks your machine before choosing a transcription path. It shows the model, size, storage location, and network work clearly, then waits for your approval.

small · start quickly

The smallest and fastest option. Good for a first episode or modest hardware, though names, accents, and noisy recordings may need more correction.

turbo · balanced

The usual recommendation for newer Apple Silicon Macs. It can also work well on Windows with a suitable GPU.

large-v3 · accuracy first

Better suited to name-heavy shows, strong accents, or uneven recordings. It is the largest download and uses more time and memory.

Apple Silicon Mac uses MLX Whisper; Intel Mac and Windows use faster-whisper.

Already have a rough transcript? Skip Whisper and the model download—send the text as it is.

Local by default.

FAQ

A few things worth knowing
before you install.

Model choice, file storage, speaker names, and protected media—answered plainly.

Will my audio be uploaded?

Not by default. When a local transcription tool is available, the audio stays on your machine. If a cloud service is needed, you will see the service and the data it requires before anything is sent.

Does `small` reduce transcription quality?

Sometimes, especially with names, strong accents, or background noise. The tradeoff is a smaller download and faster run. Start there for a trial, then move to `turbo` or `large-v3` if the recording needs more accuracy.

Where are models stored?

In your operating system's user cache—not inside the episode folder or skill folder. The exact location is shown before download.

I already have a rough transcript. Do I need a model?

No. The skill keeps the rough text, splits it safely, and goes straight to checking and editing.

Will it identify every speaker?

Not reliably in every recording. When names can be verified, they are used. Otherwise the transcript keeps a neutral label such as Speaker A for you to review.

Will it bypass a paywall or protected media?

No. It stops at sign-ins, payment gates, DRM, and platform protection, then asks you for media or text you have the right to use.

Does it publish the transcript when finished?

No. It creates local Markdown and HTML files, then stops. Publishing is your decision.
